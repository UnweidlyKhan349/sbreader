About SB Reader

SB Reader is a free practice site for the National Science Bowl and other Science Bowl tournaments, for high school and middle school teams. It has real tossups and bonuses in biology, chemistry, physics, math, earth & space science and energy, taken from regional, nationals and invitational packets and from practice sets written by school teams. There's no account and nothing to install; starred questions and settings are saved in your browser.

How to play

  • Play: choose a mode in the settings on the right (a set and its packets, random questions, or your starred questions), pick categories, then press Next. The question types itself onto the screen; press Space to buzz, type your answer and press Enter. Tossups give you 4 seconds to buzz after the question finishes, bonuses 20, and you then have 10 seconds to answer.
  • Multiplayer: join a public lobby, create a room and share its link, or join a friend's room with its 5-letter code. The first player to buzz locks everyone else out until they've answered.
  • Database: search every question by text, answer or tournament, and filter by subject, round, type, format, level and tournament. The set list shows every tournament in the bank.
  • Starred questions: star a question (☆) in the Database or in the question history, then choose Starred Questions in Play's mode menu to practice just those.
  • Account (optional): log in to keep your starred questions, stats and settings on every device. Without one, everything is saved in your browser.

Shortcuts are ignored while you're typing in a text box (except Enter). While an enlarged figure is open, Esc closes it.

How answers are graded

Answers are checked automatically, following Science Bowl conventions. If the grader gets one wrong, use I was wrong / I was right (or Q) to flip it.

  • Multiple choice: give the letter (W, X, Y or Z) or the exact text of the choice.
  • Short answer: capitalisation, punctuation and spacing don't matter, and the packet's "accept" and "do not accept" notes are followed.
  • Numbers: compared whole, with their sign. Scientific notation can be typed as 3.2 x 10^23, 3.2*10^23 or 3.2e23. A decimal matches a fraction in lowest terms with the same value (0.5 = 1/2), and units may be written out or abbreviated (10 joules = 10 J).
  • "Identify all" questions: give the item numbers in any form (1 and 3, 13, 1, 3). Ranking questions must be in the right order.
  • Multi-part answers: every part must be given, separated by commas, semicolons or "and".

Sources

Questions come from publicly posted practice packets on scibowl.live, tournament packets from scibowl.stanford.edu and oly.mehvix.com, the official NSB regional sample sets from science.osti.gov, the Science Bowl Periphery packet archive and database, released ISOBowl Premier packets, and a personal archive. Where a packet is online, each question links back to it.
