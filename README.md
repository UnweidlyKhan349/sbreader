# SB Reader: free Science Bowl practice questions, buzzer trainer and multiplayer

[![Live site](https://img.shields.io/badge/live%20site-sbreader.github.io-5b8cff)](https://sbreader.github.io/)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-MIT-35c281)](LICENSE)
[![CI](https://github.com/UnweidlyKhan349/sbreader/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/UnweidlyKhan349/sbreader/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

**[Open SB Reader →](https://sbreader.github.io/)**

SB Reader is a free practice site for the **National Science Bowl** (NSB) and other Science Bowl tournaments, for high school and middle school teams. It has over 100,000 real tossups and bonuses in biology, chemistry, physics, math, earth & space science and energy, taken from regional, nationals and invitational packets (MIT, Stanford, Yale and many more) and from practice sets written by school teams. Search them in the catalog, drill alone with a buzzer and automatic grading, or play against friends in an online multiplayer room. No account or install needed.

Created by **Henry Lu** ([UnweidlyKhan349](https://github.com/UnweidlyKhan349) on GitHub).

Under the hood it's a static site (a searchable catalog, a solo buzzer trainer, a peer-to-peer multiplayer mode, and starred questions kept per device) written in plain HTML/CSS/JS, with no build step, JavaScript framework or server of its own to run, so it can be deployed straight to GitHub Pages or any static host. The optional extras use a [Supabase](https://supabase.com) project: accounts, the Multiplayer room list and relay, and question reports, through the database schema in `supabase/schema.sql`, plus one small Edge Function that hands out TURN logins for Multiplayer (`supabase/functions/turn-credentials`). Without a project everything else still works. Its look follows [QB Reader](https://www.qbreader.org/), the quizbowl reader: Bootstrap 5.3's stylesheet (from the jsDelivr CDN at a fixed version, with an integrity hash) with Light, Night and Auto themes.

**Current question bank: 102,375 questions from 114 sets** (55,536 tossups / 46,839 bonuses; 63,530 short answer / 38,845 multiple choice), including **567 visual questions** whose figures (569 images) are shown with the question. Every question links to where it's published, and 60,162 of those links open the packet at the question's page or line.

## Pages and features

Every page shares one navbar, laid out like QB Reader's: the **SB**Reader wordmark, then **Play** (`solo.html`), **Multiplayer**, **Database ▾** (Search = `catalog.html`, Set List) and **About**, with a theme menu (Light / Night / Auto) and **Log in** on the right (your name, once logged in). Below 992 px it folds into a ☰ menu. `js/site.js`, loaded in every page's `<head>`, puts the saved theme (`localStorage` `sb_theme`; Light on a first visit, Auto follows the system setting) on `<html data-bs-theme>` before the first paint, sets the browser bar colour (`theme-color`) to match, so a Night page never flashes white, and wires the dropdowns, the ☰ button and the current-page highlight.

### `index.html` (Home)
A welcome line and four cards, as on QB Reader's home page: the question reader (Play, Multiplayer, and a Tips & Instructions button), the question database (live question and tournament counts read from `meta.json` alone, with Search and Set List), the sources, and the code, changelog and licence. Once the page has loaded and the browser is idle, it prefetches the question bank's files into the browser cache at low priority, so the page you open next doesn't wait for the download (skipped when the browser reports Save-Data or a 2G connection).

### `about.html` (About), `tips.html` (Tips & Instructions) and `set-list.html` (Set List)
Tips & Instructions is laid out like QB Reader's: a heading and lead line, then cards two to a row (one on a phone), each a short list: Getting Started, Searching the Database, Accounts & Progress, Help & Community, and Multiplayer. Grading and the keyboard shortcuts are on About, which it links. The home page's Sources card (a blue Tips & Instructions button, as on QB Reader) and the About page link to it.

About is laid out like QB Reader's: a lead line; paragraphs on where the packets come from (report a question, or open an issue) and contributing; then questions as headings, in QB Reader's order where they apply (Why SB Reader?, What does making an account do?, Why do I need to confirm my email?, Does it cost money?, What do I do if someone is causing trouble in a room?, Where can I request a set?, Can I get a copy of all the questions?), then How are answers graded?, What are the keyboard shortcuts? and Packet List (the Set List). Set List follows QB Reader's: a "Search set names" box (every word must appear in the name; kept in the URL as `?q=`) over a table of every tournament with its level, approximate date and number of packets, tossups and bonuses, newest first; click a column heading to sort by it, again to reverse (QB Reader's filled carets mark the sorted column). The counts are stored per tournament in `meta.json` (`count`, `tossups`, `bonuses`, `packets`) and refreshed with `node tools/meta-counts.js`; a test checks they match the bank. As on QB Reader, each name opens the set's page (`set.html?set=<slug>`): "View <set> here:", Back to all sets, the tournament's **Source packets** link (`SBData.tournamentSourceUrl`: a Google Drive folder a packet links to, or the place 70% or more of its packet links point into: its ISOBowl page, the NSB's sample-question page, a GitHub repository, scibowldb or the Periphery database, or a folder that lists its files; a tournament's `source` in `meta.json` overrides it; when the packets are only separate files, "Source packets: 1 2 3 …" links each one), and a table of its packets (#, name, toss-ups, bonuses). Each packet opens `packet.html?set=<slug>&packet=N`: every question in the packet in reading order ("1. **TOSS-UP** Physics *Short Answer*", the question, its choices and figure), and its ANSWER line, under a **Source packet** link to the original. These pages load only that tournament's file (`SBData.loadSet`). The catalog still takes `catalog.html?tournament=<slug>`.

### `catalog.html` (Database › Search)
- Browse and search every question, with a debounce on input. Three radio buttons under the filters (QB Reader's search type, but not a dropdown) pick where to look: **All text** (everything a card shows: the question, its W/X/Y/Z choices, the answer with its "also accept" and "do not accept" entries, and the tournament name), **Question only** (the question and its choices) or **Answer only** (the answer and its accepted alternates); matches are marked only there. **Ignore word order** (on by default, as on QB Reader's database) means each word typed must appear somewhere, but not next to each other or in order (`chloride sodium` finds "sodium chloride", and `aves photosynthesis` finds AVES questions about photosynthesis); off, the words must appear together as typed. **Exact words** (off by default) makes each word typed count only as a whole word: `p waves` finds "P-waves" and "… p … waves" but not "capillary waves" (and not "p wave"), `rna` leaves out "mRNA", `ion` leaves out "solution". A word ends at anything that isn't a letter or a digit, so a hyphen, an exponent mark or a bracket ends it (`h2o` is one word; `h2` isn't in it). Searching is never case-sensitive, spacing is evened out, and what a keyboard doesn't have is typed plain (`SBData.searchFold`): `schrodinger` finds "Schrödinger", `hooke's` finds "Hooke’s", `p-wave` finds "P–wave". Each question's search text is built once, in small batches while the browser is idle after the page loads, and reused. Matches are highlighted in the results (the whole phrase when word order counts, whole words only with Exact words), and exponent markup is ignored, so `7100` finds `7^100`. `npm test` searches for every question by the first words of its own question, choices, answer and accepts, in each scope, and checks a set of searches against a separate word-by-word check of the whole bank.
- Filters: subject chips, a dual-handle **round-range slider** that spans only the rounds the picked tournaments have (all 20 with none picked; a range that reached the top keeps reaching it), with an "Include unlabeled" checkbox (26,747 questions have no round number), question type (Tossup/Bonus), format (Short Answer/Multiple Choice), level (High School/Middle School), **visual questions** (Visual/Text only; selecting neither means both), and a tournament picker: a searchable multi-select dropdown that lists the selected tournaments first. The layout follows QB Reader's database page: a query bar with a **Search** button (typing searches as you go too) and a green **Random** button, as on QB Reader, which shows 40 random questions from those matching the filters (no pages; changing a filter draws a new 40, Search goes back to the full list), the tournament picker beside a **year** slider ("Years 1993-2026": the tournaments' years, from each one's `date` in `meta.json`) and, to its right, the round slider (under the years on a phone), the filter toggles, and switches for "Include questions with no round number" and **Hide answers** (remembered per device as `sb_hide_answers`; hidden answers sit behind a Show answer link on each card). There's also a "Clear all filters" link.
- With no tournament picked, results list the newest tournaments first (by each tournament's approximate `date` in `meta.json`), each in packet order; picking tournaments lists them A–Z. Packet order is: by tournament, then round (unlabeled rounds last), then question number, each tossup before its bonus. Filtering to MIT 2023 shows Round 1 from question 1, then Round 2, and so on. NSB Regs, whose rounds are numbered within each set, shows Round 1 of every set before Round 2 (`SBData.sortByPacketOrder`).
- Above the results, as on QB Reader: "Showing 40 of 2111 results (0.03 seconds)". The subject boxes share the full row equally.
- 40 results per page, with Bootstrap pagination (first, last and nearby pages) and a jump-to-page box. Changing page scrolls back up to the first result, as on QB Reader.
- Each result is a card (`SBData.questionCard`, shared with the stats and starred pages; numbered statements each on their own line), laid out like QB Reader's: the header reads "AVES 2 | RR 1 | Question 12 | Physics | Tossup | Multiple Choice" on the left, bold and underlined, a link to the original packet when there is one (a visual tossup adds "| Visual"; a bonus with a figure is a "Visual Bonus"), and the star (Bootstrap Icons' star, filled once starred) on the right; the body starts with the question's number ("12. ...") and has the question, its figure(s) (click to enlarge), MC choices and the answer line (with inline "also accept"), with **Stats | Report** on the answer line's right. There's no footer.
- **Stats** (`js/question-stats.js`), as QB Reader's "Tossup Stats": how everyone has done on the question, from the plays recorded in Play and Multiplayer (see [Question stats](#question-stats)): how many heard it, how many answered right (+4), wrong (0), with an interrupt (−4) or not at all, the average celerity of the right answers, total points and points per question heard.

### `solo.html` (Play)
- **Layout**, as on QB Reader's play page: the question on the left; on the right a settings sidebar with a large timer readout (seconds and tenths), the question settings, the switches and the reading speed; and a button bar pinned to the bottom of the screen: **Next** (Skip while a question is being read), Pause, the ⚙ settings button, and **Buzz** on the right, at their natural widths and all the same height, as on QB Reader (the icons are Bootstrap Icons' pause-fill and gear). Above the question a statline reads "3/1 with 6 questions seen (8 pts, celerity: 0.805)", with the tournament, round and question number on the right, separated by spaces, with how many questions the packet has ("Prometheus 2021 RR 3 Bonus 19"; when reading a set by packet, also how many questions the packet has, "Olympus 2022 RR 1 Toss-up 19 of 25"; the packet's highest question number, so sets that number tossups and bonuses in one run read right). There's no setup screen and no instructions to read first: the page opens on this view and the first Next starts a session.
- **What's read out**: each question starts the way a moderator reads it, "**TOSS-UP** Physics *Short Answer*" (or **BONUS** / **VISUAL BONUS**; the kind in semi-bold, the format in italics) on its own line, then the question, typed out with the question instead of separate subject/type/format chips. A rule with some space above it separates the current question from the history.
- **Two modes** (Random Questions and Starred Questions; Select by Set Name was removed on November 13, 2026), chosen at the top of the sidebar, as in QB Reader. The page always opens on Random Questions (unless the link asks for starred ones). With nothing to play, an alert says "No questions found" ("No starred questions." in Starred mode, whose sidebar then says just that). In Starred mode a question unstarred since the session began isn't read again, and when the last star has gone, Next (or `n`) gives the same alert and leaves the last question on screen. The number of questions that match sits under the subjects.
  - **Random Questions**: the tournaments picker (none picked means all), a **year range** slider ("Year range: 1993 - 2026", as on QB Reader) and, under it, the round slider; questions are dealt round-robin across the chosen subjects (`SBData.stratifiedQueue`), so subjects stay evenly mixed until the smaller ones run out, and no question comes up twice.
  - **Starred Questions**: only the questions you've starred, in random order. `solo.html?mode=starred` (the home page's "Starred Questions" button) opens straight into it.
- **Subjects**: one toggle per subject, right in the sidebar, styled like QB Reader's category boxes (full-size buttons, red outline, square corners, filled red when on; hovering doesn't fill them). They apply in Set Name and Random modes and are hidden in Starred mode. The Database's subject filters look the same.
- **Switches**, as in QB Reader (all together under a rule, below the subjects), saved per device (`localStorage` `sb_solo_settings`, with the mode, set and categories):
  - **Allow rebuzzes**: after a wrong answer you can buzz again on the same question (the reading carries on); off, a wrong answer ends it.
  - **Type to answer** (on by default): off, as on QB Reader, you grade yourself: Buzz turns into **Reveal** while the 10-second answer clock runs, and Reveal (or the clock running out) shows the answer, counted as right; **I was wrong** (`q`) flips it.
  - **Enable timer**: off, nothing is timed: no buzz window and no answer countdown.
- **Question history**: each finished question becomes a collapsed card when the next one starts, as in QB Reader: the header is just the answer (no Correct / Incorrect chip) and a star; click it, or press `K` for the newest, to open the question, its answer line and the packet link. `T` stars or unstars the newest; stars sync to your account when you're logged in.
- **Reveal instead of text-to-speech**: questions and their answer choices appear on screen word by word (`js/reveal.js`), timed with QB Reader's formula (the "TOSS-UP Physics Short Answer" header shows at once and takes no time, so the question starts reading immediately, as QB Reader's does): after each word the reveal waits (ln(word length) + 1, plus 2.5 at the end of a sentence or 1.5 after a comma) × 0.9 × (140 − reading speed) ms. The reading speed runs from 0 to 100 in steps of 5, 50 by default, as on QB Reader. Math and formulas type as they'll look (superscripts and subscripts are there from the first character). The reveal position is computed from wall-clock time rather than tick counts, so it doesn't fall behind in a backgrounded browser tab.
- **Figures**: a visual question shows its figure (between the question text and the choices) as soon as the question starts. As on QB Reader, no space is reserved for text still to come: the page grows as the question types out (the figure moves down with it, and each answer choice appears when its text starts), and the next question's images are preloaded while you answer this one. Click a figure to open it full-size; `Esc` or a click closes it.
- **Celerity**, as QB Reader shows it: how much of the question was still unread when you buzzed, averaged over your correct answers, from 0 to 1 ("celerity: 0.805"; higher is earlier). A buzz after the question has finished counts as 0. It follows overrides.
- **Answer line**: "ANSWER: LITHIUM-ION (also accept: ...) (do not accept: LITHIUM)", all on one line (in the Database and history cards too).
- **Source link**: once the question is over, the label above it ("AVES 2 RR 3 Toss-up 12") becomes a link to the original packet, underlined but still in the text colour, opened at the question's page when it's a PDF. Multiplayer sends the link with the answer, not with the question, so nobody can open the packet early.
- **Timers**: a 4 s buzz window for tossups and 20 s for bonuses once the reveal finishes, then 10 s to type an answer. Once someone has buzzed on the question (a wrong answer, with the question going on for the others or for a rebuzz), the buzz window is never under 5 s: it picks up at what was left when the buzz came, or 5 s if that's more, so a wrong answer in the last second still leaves time to buzz (`AFTER_BUZZ_MS`; Multiplayer too). The timer never turns red.
- **Grading**: automatic (see [Answer grading](#answer-grading)), with a manual override (`Q`, or the link at the right of the "Correct sqrt 137" line: **I was wrong** after a Correct, **I was right** after an Incorrect). +4 for a correct answer; −4 for a wrong buzz before the question has finished, at most once per question.
- **Changing settings mid-session**: every setting and the reading speed apply live; a change only rebuilds the not-yet-asked part of the queue, so you won't see a question twice.
- **Settings sidebar**: open beside the question on wide screens; ⚙ or `E` hides and shows it. On screens narrower than 992 px it sits above the question and starts folded, showing only the (smaller) timer until ⚙ opens it.

### `multiplayer.html` (Multiplayer)
- **Lobby page**, as on QB Reader's: "You can invite friends to join you by sharing the URL to your room. There are N active players in M active rooms." Under it, **Create/Join a Room** with one name box (a random name like `quantum-wombat` as the placeholder) and **Go**, which joins that room or opens it if nobody's in it, and **Make room private** and **Make room controlled** checkboxes. Then "Try one of these rooms": the built-in rooms (General, Math, Physics, Biology, Chemistry, Earth & Space), and "Or, check out a public room": every public room as "name - 12 players - 3 online", with a **Show empty rooms** checkbox. The list is read once when the page opens; there's no refresh button and the page doesn't update itself. The last list this browser saw is drawn at once (`localStorage` `sb_mp_rooms_cache`) and replaced by the current one within a moment; it's fetched straight from the project's REST API as the page loads, without waiting for the Supabase library. Picking a room (or Go) opens it on the same page, without reloading: the lobby has already opened this player's connection and fetched the TURN logins, so only the room itself has to be reached. There's no name box: you get a random name, which you change inside the room.
- **Room links**: a room is just its URL, `multiplayer.html?room=<name>` (lowercase letters, digits and dashes, up to 32), or `?lobby=<key>` for a built-in room. Opening it shows the room straight away, with "Connecting to <name>…", and joins it (back to the lobby, with the reason, if that fails). As on QB Reader, the room's name isn't shown above the question; it's in the page title and the address bar, and you share the room by sharing that URL.
- **Public and private**: a room made with "Make room private" (`&private=true`) isn't listed. Anyone in a room can switch **Public room** in its settings. Built-in rooms are always public.
- **Room list and player counts** come from two tables in the optional Supabase project (`mp_rooms`, `mp_players`; see [Accounts](#accounts)). Every player in a room writes a heartbeat every 30 s (and on each settings change) with their name, score, correct, incorrect and neg counts, and celerity; "online" means seen in the last 90 s. How long players and rooms last follows QB Reader, which keeps them in its server's memory and restarts the server every day at 08:00 UTC. There is no timer per player or per room; that hour is the cut-off (`SBMpRules.lastRestart`, and `mp_last_restart` in the schema). **Offline players**: after they leave, players are kept with their score, offline, until the next 08:00 UTC, and come back as themselves (score and all) if they rejoin from the same browser before it; a player who leaves without having heard or answered a question (`SBMpRules.hasActivity`) is taken off the list at once. "Players" counts the players a room still has. **Rooms**: every room, public or private, played in or not, stays after its players leave until the next 08:00 UTC, when it is deleted with its owner and bans (an empty room is never removed after 90 seconds); the built-in rooms are kept, and start each day with no players. Opening a deleted room's name starts it afresh, and whoever opens it becomes its owner. Players who are in a room at 08:00 UTC keep playing with their scores (QB Reader's restart disconnects everyone; there is no server here to restart). Nothing runs on a clock: the functions that read the list leave out what has lapsed, and every heartbeat deletes it. Without Supabase the built-in rooms still work and show their online count (asked of the room directly). When no public room is showing, the list just says "No public rooms."
- **In a room** the layout matches Play: the question on the left; a sidebar with the timer, **Show offline players**, the players, your username, and the room settings; and the bottom bar with Start / Next / Skip, Pause, **Chat**, ⚙ and Buzz.
- **Players**, as in QB Reader: one row per player (names in the text colour) with their points in a pill (green when online), yours highlighted; offline players are hidden unless "Show offline players" is on. Click a player to open a popover beside the list, as on QB Reader, with their name, points, correct answers, incorrect answers, negs (wrong answers given while the question was still being read, which cost 4 points), accuracy, celerity (0 to 1) and whether they're online; click anywhere else or press Esc to close it. Beside every other online player's name, a small ▾ menu holds **Votekick**, as on QB Reader.
- **Room owners**, as on QB Reader: a named room belongs to the browser that created it. As on QB Reader, the owner counts only while the room is private: there they have QB Reader's crown before their name on the leaderboard, can't be vote kicked, and their ▾ menu on other players adds **Mute** / **Unmute** (a muted player's chat doesn't reach the room; they see "You are muted in this room") and **Ban** (the player is sent back to the lobby with "You were banned from that room by its owner.", taken off the leaderboard, and turned away by their player ID for 30 minutes, as on QB Reader and like a vote kick; offline players can be banned too). A public room has no crown, mutes or owner tools, and the built-in rooms (always public) have no owner at all. There's no server to vouch for anyone, so the owner proves who they are with a signing key only their browser holds (ECDSA P-256 through WebCrypto, `localStorage` `sb_mp_owner_key`): the room keeps the public half, and the owner signs `<room>|<their connection ID>|<time>` when joining, which the coordinator checks (good for a minute, and only for the connection that sent it). With Supabase, the owner's key, the bans and the controlled switch are kept in `mp_rooms`, so a room comes back with the same owner after everyone has left; without it, whoever opens a room that isn't running becomes its owner until it empties. Ownership is per browser: another device (or cleared site data) isn't the owner.
- **Vote kicks**, with QB Reader's rules: only a player who has answered a question correctly can start one (at most once every 90 seconds); it passes with three quarters of the players online, and never fewer than two votes. Starting it, each vote and the kick are logged in the room history ("Alice started a vote kick against Carol (1/2)"). The kicked player is sent back to the lobby ("You were vote kicked from that room.") and turned away if they try to come back within 30 minutes (by their player ID; every player keeps the list, so it survives the coordinator leaving).
- **Username**: the box under the players, with a person icon. Change it and press Enter or click away; the room shows "Bob changed their username to Robert". Names are made unique within the room. It's remembered per device (`sb_mp_name`).
- **Room history**, as in QB Reader, newest first: "Bob joined the game", "Alice started the game" (but not who started each next question), "Robert skipped the question", settings changes ("Robert enabled multiple buzzes", "Alice changed the subjects to Math, Physics"), and buzzes. When someone buzzes, a line with a **Buzz** badge (**Answer** on a bonus, as on QB Reader) and their name (no separate "Bob buzzed" line) shows what they're typing as they type it; when they submit, it gets a green **Correct** or red **Incorrect** badge (white text on both). Finished questions become collapsed question cards in the same list (`K` opens the newest, `T` stars it).
- **Chat**: unlike QB Reader, chat works in every room, public ones included. Chat (or `Enter`, or `/`) opens a message box above the question; messages appear in the room history.
- **Room settings**, editable by anyone in the room, as in QB Reader: random questions by filter (tournaments, the year range and the round slider under it; letting go of either slider tells the room, "changed the years to 2015-2026"), the subject toggles, and switches for **Public room**, **Controlled room**, **Allow rebuzzes** (multiple buzzes; off, a wrong answer locks that player out for the rest of the question, the room's owner included), **Allow skips** (off, nobody, the room's owner included, can skip a question while it's read or open for buzzing; in a room with the timer off, anyone can move on once the reading is over, with **Next**, since nothing else would end the question, and that isn't logged as a skip), **Enable timer** (off, nobody is rushed to buzz; switched on once a question has been read, the buzz countdown starts), and the reading speed. A built-in subject room keeps its subject. Changes go to the coordinator, are broadcast to everyone and logged in the history.
- **Controlled rooms**, as on QB Reader: only the room's owner can switch **Controlled room** on, and only in a private room (a room is never both public and controlled: **Make room controlled** in the lobby opens it private, and **Public room** is locked while it's controlled). While it's on only the owner can change the settings (everyone else's are greyed out; a change sent anyway is refused by the coordinator and undone on the sender's screen).
- **Scoring**: +4 for a correct answer. A wrong answer costs −4 if you buzzed before the question finished revealing, and 0 otherwise. Players can override their own graded answer (`Q`), except that a wrong answer can't be overridden to correct once someone else has scored the question.
- **How rooms work without a server**: each room's door is a fixed PeerJS ID (`sbowl-room-<name>`, or `sbowl-pub-<key>` for a built-in room). In the room list a built-in room is recorded as `pub-<key>` (`pub-all` is General), so a room's name can't start with `pub-` (`pub-all` becomes `all`), and the database treats only the built-in names (`mp_is_builtin`) as public and permanent. Go first tries to claim that ID and open the room; if it's taken, the player goes in through it. A failed join goes round again after a random 0.5–1.5 s wait, up to four times. To be quick, everything that can happen at once does: this player's connection to the broker, the claim on the door, the room's record from Supabase (`mp_room`) and the question bank all start together. Joining someone else's room doesn't wait for the question bank at all (the room sends what's on screen); whoever opens a room waits for it only before the first question, and a player still loading it who'd take over as coordinator does so once it arrives.
  - **Rooms belong to nobody**: every player is connected to every other, and anyone can leave, the player who created the room included. The game goes on at once when someone leaves, mid-question included. Leaving takes effect as soon as the others hear the goodbye; a player who drops off without one is noticed through the connection checks, in about 3–8 seconds.
  - Every player keeps the whole live game: players and scores, the players who've left, the question log, the chat, the settings, and where the current question stands. Buzzes, timers and grading need one player to rule on them, so the longest-standing player still connected does (the "coordinator", `SBMpRules.pickCoordinator`), and every message it sends carries its term. When the coordinator leaves, everyone already agrees on who's next, and that player continues from its own copy (`takeOver`). Messages from a newer term win (`SBMpRules.acceptsCoordinator`).
  - **Scores follow the player**, not the connection: each browser has a player ID (`localStorage` `sb_mp_pid`) that it sends with `join`, and a player who leaves is kept by that ID. With Supabase, a room's players and scores also outlast everyone leaving: the next person to open it loads them from `mp_players`, negs and celerity included. (A project set up from a copy of `schema.sql` older than November 6, 2026 lacks those columns: run the file again to add them; until then everything else is still saved.) ## Answer grading

All grading lives in `js/answer-check.js` (exposed as `window.SBAnswer`). The core is `checkAnswer(question, userInput)`.

**The principle.** The grader does what a Science Bowl moderator does with a spoken answer. It **accepts** what a moderator would take without asking: the key said another way, with nothing left out and nothing wrong. It marks **close** what a moderator would prompt on or look at twice: a misspelling, or the key with more said around it; the player sees "Close" and decides with `Q`. It **rejects** the rest: part of the key missing, a different number, sign or term. Every rule below is that principle applied to one kind of answer, and a new case is settled by asking which of the three a moderator would do. Multiple choice is the exception: the letter or the exact choice text, and nothing else.

**Normalization** (`normalize()`): uppercases, unifies apostrophes, and strips punctuation. It also maps symbols to words so either form matches the other:
- `√` ↔ `SQRT`, `∛` ↔ `CBRT`, `π`/`Π` ↔ `PI`
- "square root(s) of X" → `SQRT X`, and "cube root(s) of X" → `CBRT X`

So `128 π` = `128 pi`, `cube root of 35` = `cbrt 35` = `cbrt(35)`, and `square root of 16` = `sqrt 16`.

### How a key is read

- **Accept and reject lists** (`a.ac`, `a.rj`) are honored.
- **Multiple choice**: accepts the letter (W/X/Y/Z) or the full choice text.
- **"Identify all" and list questions**: the item count is detected from the question's item markers, `1) … 2) …`, `1. … 2. …` or `1: … 2: …`, or, when the stem has no such run, Roman numerals in capitals, `I. … II. … III. …` (so `3`, `iii` or `3 only` all match a key of `III ONLY`, `1 and 3` matches `I AND III`, and a key written in numbers takes numerals too) (a colon counts only with a space after it, so times like `3:30` and ratios like `1:2` don't, and neither does a label such as `row 1:` or `Step 1:`), and the answer is canonicalized, so `1 and 3`, `13`, and `1, 3` all match. Item *names* are resolved to their numbers (e.g. "diatoms radiolarians"). **"All but X"** phrasing is resolved by excluding the named or numbered item(s). A key that answers each item in turn (`1: RIGHT; 2: LEFT`, `1) +, 2) –, 3) –`, or a two-question key like `1) Carboxylic acid; 2) A, C, B`) isn't a choice of items, so `all` or `123` doesn't pass for it; a key that names items by number (`2) AND 3)`, `3) SA NODE; 1) AV NODE; …`) still does.` forms counted, so about 500 questions written with `1:` (most of them Mira Loma's) had no item count, and the 19 rankings among them accepted any order.
- **"Name any N" keys** are stored in one form, `ANY 2 OF: EARTH, TITAN, PLUTO, TRITON`: any N distinct listed items pass, in any order, and more than N pass if every one is listed; an unlisted or repeated item fails. A parenthetical in a listed item is another name for it (`HEXAHEDRON (CUBE)`). (The grader still reads the forms packets print, `(ACCEPT 3) …`, `{ANY 2} …`, `… (any 3)`, so a new scrape grades before it is tidied.)
- **Multi-part answers** (a key with top-level `;` parts, e.g. `FIRST QUARTER; FULL`): every part must be given, and each is matched on its own. The typed answer can be split with `;`, `,`, `and`, `then`, or nothing at all (`first quarter full moon`), and a part can carry one extra word (`full moon` for `FULL`). A part's label or number can be left off (`25` for `MEDIAN = 25`, `magnons` for `1) MAGNONS`). Order is enforced for "respectively", ranking, or sequence questions and for labelled parts. Otherwise any order is accepted, as it is when the packet says "either order". A single-part accept is only an alternate for that one part (`LH` for `LUTEINIZING HORMONE`), and `1ST QUARTER | FULL MOON` gives an alternate for each part. Before this change, a single part often passed as a whole answer through the fuzzy fallback (`full moon` alone) or through a lone per-part accept (`LH`, `COPPER`), while `first quarter and full moon` was marked wrong. Checked across the corpus: a main answer, a whole-answer accept, or the parts joined by `,` or `and` now always pass (531 multi-part questions). The only inputs that no longer pass are single parts and out-of-order answers to ordered questions.
- **Multi-part part labels**: a part may be labelled `1)`, `2:`, `3 -` or a roman `I)`, `II)` (up to IX, since `X)` is a choice letter), and the labels can be left off. A part keyed as a multiple-choice letter and its choice (`X) B-V INCREASES …`, `W ((R)-METHYL 3-HYDROXYBUTANOATE)`) takes the letter, the choice text, or both.
- **Ranking questions** keep order (a permutation) instead of being treated as sets, however the numbers are typed (`132`, `1, 3, 2`, `1 and 3 and 2`). A ranking is recognised from "order/rank/arrange/sort … the following", "order them", "in increasing/chronological order", "in order of increasing …", "by increasing/decreasing …", "put/place/list … in (the correct) order", "chronologically" or "from earliest … to …", but never in an "identify/select all" question, and "second-order", "in order to", "in order for" and "in order that" don't count. A ranking can also be typed with the item names in order (`SA node, AV node, bundle of His`), as long as every item is named once and the names stay distinct once normalized (`NO2^-` and `NO2^+` don't, so those take numbers). The ordering can come from the key or an accept (`2/5; 0.402; 42%` with accept `3, 2, 1`). Before this, a ranking stem that didn't put "the following" right after the verb was graded as a set, and even recognised rankings accepted any order typed with commas or spaces (`3, 2, 1` passed for `1, 3, 2`); only a bare digit run like `321` was checked. A typed ranking has to be item numbers alone, so `128/3` or `vertices = 12, edges = 30` no longer reads as `1, 2, 3`, and an extra item (`1, 3, 2, 4`) is wrong.
- **List answers** (a key whose items are joined by commas or `AND`, e.g. `CARBON DIOXIDE AND MOLECULAR NITROGEN`): each typed item is matched to a key item, in any order. An accept with the same number of items lends its items to the key items in the same places when it is written all in formulas (`CO2 AND N2`) or renames just one item (`CARBON DIOXIDE AND NITROGEN`), so names, formulas and alternates mix: `nitrogen and carbon dioxide`, `CO2 and nitrogen` and `co2 n` all pass. Formulas are read case-sensitively (`Fe`, `CO2`, `Na^+`), so an all-caps second answer such as `ALUMINUM AND SILICON` never mixes with the key. A list on an ordered question has to be given in its order: ranking wording, "respectively", a matrix written in rows, an ordered pair, or a point's coordinates. `magnesium, iron` passes "the only metal in forsterite and the only metal in fayalite, respectively" and `iron, magnesium` doesn't. Before this, only `;` keys were held to their order, so 419 comma or AND lists on ordered questions passed when reversed (`water, aluminum, lead` for "order … from largest to smallest increase in temperature": `LEAD, ALUMINUM, WATER`). Checked across the corpus: every key and accept still passes in its own order, and the only answers that now pass where they didn't are formula and mixed forms of substance lists and number sets with signs in another order (`5, -2` for eigenvalues `-2, 5`).

### Accepted: the key said another way

- **Word order, stop words and plurals**: `jupiter saturn` = `SATURN AND JUPITER`, `the troposphere` = `TROPOSPHERE`, `mitochondrion` = `MITOCHONDRIA`.
- **Accents are ignored**: `schrodinger` is `SCHRÖDINGER`, `huckel` `HÜCKEL`, `le chatelier` `LE CHÂTELIER`.
- **Possessives**: a possessive matches with or without its ending (`hookes law`, `hooke law` and `hooke's law` for `HOOKE'S LAW`). `michaelson` is taken for `MICHELSON`, the one name so often written that way.
- **Numbers as words and roman numerals**: `0` = `ZERO`, `twelve protons` = `12 PROTONS` (zero to twenty, either way round); after a stage, type or complex name a roman numeral equals its digit: `meiosis 2` = `MEIOSIS II`, `type 1a` = `TYPE IA`, `photosystem 2` = `PHOTOSYSTEM II`.
- **Abbreviations**: `ER`, `RER` and `SER` read as endoplasmic reticulum, rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum (so `rough ER` matches `ROUGH ENDOPLASMIC RETICULUM`). The table is in `normalize()` in `js/answer-check.js`.
- **Exponent markup is ignored**: `x^2` = `x2` and `e^(-x)` = `e-x`, so answer keys that gained `^` in the superscript repair still accept what students type.
- **Spacing-insensitive match**: answers that contain a letter and are identical once whitespace is removed count as equal, so `SiO2` matches an answer key of `SI O2` and `MgCl2` matches `MgCl 2` (PDF extraction scattered spaces through formulas). Punctuation is kept, so `1.5` never meets `15`, and bare numbers are excluded because spacing can matter there. The same rule applies to reject lists. Checked against the whole corpus: grading every short-answer question with another question's answer gives exactly as many acceptances as before (no new false positives).
- **What a keyboard has**: a one-character fraction is its slash form (`1/2` or `0.5` for `½`, `2 1/2` for `2½`); a Greek letter, `ħ` and `∞` are typed by name (`omega` for `ω`, `delta H` for `ΔH`, `sin theta`, `hbar`, `infinity`), with spacing around the name not counting; and the letter `x` between numbers is a times sign (`67 x 101` for `67 × 101`).
- **Fractions and decimals**: a decimal and a fraction with exactly the same value match, so `0.5` = `1/2`, `0.75` = `3/4`, `5.5` = `11/2`, also with a trailing unit or symbol (`0.5 pi` for `1/2 PI`, a bare `0.5` for `1/2 METERS`). Nothing else is compared by value: two fractions, two decimals, or a decimal and an integer are still compared as written, because packets reject unreduced fractions (`15/60` for `1/4`) and dropped significant figures (`1 × 10^-8` for `1.0 × 10^-8`). The fraction must be in lowest terms, and `0.33` is not `1/3`.
- **Units (short answer only)**: after a number, a unit may be written out or abbreviated, singular or plural: `10 joules` = `10 J`, `9.8 meters per second squared` = `9.8 m/s^2`, `5 kilojoules per mole` = `5 kJ/mol`, `100 degrees Celsius` = `100 °C`, `2.5 electron volts` = `2.5 eV`, `5 Ω` = `5 OHMS`. A different unit is still wrong, temperatures never meet coulombs or farads (`5 celsius` isn't `5 C`), and "molar" is left alone, since `M` would also read as meters. A unit's name on its own isn't its symbol (`J` doesn't pass for `JOULE`). Multiple choice is never touched: its answer must be the letter or the exact choice.
- **Bare numbers** match answer keys like `8 FACTORS`, `1080 N` or `4 TIMES AS FAST`, in a single answer or in one part of a multi-part answer, but not keys where more math follows (`20 pi`, `7 times 10^-10`, `4 OVER PI`) or a word carries the sign (`870 JOULES LESS`, `273 DEGREES BELOW ZERO`). Leading-number extraction and set comparison handle numeric answers.
- **Alternates inside the key**: a key that lists its own alternates (`ITCZ OR INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE`, `GREEN ACCEPT: EMERALD`) accepts each one, and a trailing note in parentheses can be left off (`TORRICELLI'S THEOREM (OR LAW)`). An `OR` is not split when the key is an equation or inequality, or says both answers are required (`x = 0 or x = 9 (MUST GIVE BOTH ANSWERS)`).
- **"All" said another way**: `all`, `all of them`, `all of the above`, `all of these`, `all three` / `all 3` and `every one` are one answer, so `all` passes for `ALL OF THEM` and `all of them` for `ALL` (837 keys are one or the other), whether or not the items are numbered. A count must be the right one: `all three` fails when the stem numbers four items, says "the following four", or the key is `ALL 4`. A note in brackets at the end of a key isn't part of it (`ALL (1, 2, 3)`, `1 AND 3 (KETTLE AND TARN)`).
- **"The other one"**: in an ordering of things the question itself lists (a ranking whose key names every listed item), `the other one`, `the last one`, `the remaining one` or a bare `other` stands for the one item not yet named: `p wave s wave other one` for `P-WAVE; S-WAVE; L-WAVE` ("Arrange the following 3 types of seismic waves …: S-wave; L-wave; P-wave"), `2, 3, the other one` for a numbered ranking keyed `2, 3, 1`. Only one item can be left, the rest must be right and in place, and it never applies when the question doesn't list the things (naming two of three from memory) or isn't an ordering (`checkWithOtherOne`, `rankedWithOtherOne`).
- **Exact key wins over the reject list**: typing the key character for character (`+6`) passes even when a reject entry only differs in punctuation (`6`).
- **The last word can go when the words before it say which one is meant**: a key that ends in `EXPERIMENT`, `EFFECT`, `LAW`, `PRINCIPLE`, `THEOREM`, `EQUATION`, `RULE`, `PROCESS`, `CYCLE`, `RELATION(SHIP)`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `PARADOX`, `CONJECTURE`, `POSTULATE`, `INEQUALITY`, `APPROXIMATION`, `PHENOMENON` or `LIMIT` (or its plural) is also answered without that word: `michelson-morley` for `MICHELSON-MORLEY EXPERIMENT`, `doppler` for `DOPPLER EFFECT`, `hooke's` for `HOOKE'S LAW`, `de broglie` for `DE BROGLIE RELATION`, `photoelectric` for `PHOTOELECTRIC EFFECT`, `ideal gas` for `IDEAL GAS LAW`, also inside a multi-part or list answer. The words before it are enough when they hold a name (a word in `KIND_NAMES`, the names found before these words in the bank's keys, or any possessive), are two words or more, or are one describing or long word (`greenhouse`, `pythagorean`, `uncertainty`). They aren't when what's left is a short everyday noun, a substance or an ordinal, which is a different answer by itself: `rock` isn't `ROCK CYCLE`, nor `skin` `SKIN EFFECT`, `carbon` `CARBON CYCLE`, `chain` `CHAIN RULE` or `first` `FIRST LAW`. What's left must be given in full (`pauli exclusion`, not `pauli`); a typo in it is close, and a name on the reject list stays rejected (`withoutKindWord`).

### Close: a moderator would look twice

- **Close, not correct**: a typo within a tolerance that grows with word length (`deuterosomes` for `DEUTEROSTOMES`), or the whole key plus more words (`sodium chloride` for `SODIUM`), is marked wrong with `close: true`. Solo and multiplayer then show just "Close" beside the answer, and the player overrides with `Q` if they meant it. A swap that names a different term never counts as close: `hypertonic`/`hypotonic`, `alkane`/`alkene`, `nitrate`/`nitrite`, `triphosphate`/`diphosphate`, `aerobic`/`anaerobic` and the other pairs in `CONTRAST_SWAPS`. A British or American spelling of the key (`sulphur`, `colour`, `haemoglobin`, `aluminium`) is a misspelling like any other: close when it is within the tolerance, wrong when it isn't.

### Rejected

- **Incomplete answers are wrong**: an answer that leaves out part of the key (`carbon` for `CARBON DIOXIDE`, `dwarf` for `WHITE DWARF`) gets no credit. The one exception is a last word that only names the kind of thing (see "The last word can go" above).
- **Numbers are compared whole**: `7.2` never matches `2.7`. Scientific notation can be typed with `×`, `x`, `*` or `e` (`3.2 × 10^23`, `3.2 x 10^23`, `3.2*10^23`, `3.2e23`), and the exponent's sign counts.
- **Signs on numbers**: when both answers start with a number, that leading number's sign must match. So `195 degrees` fails `-195 degrees`, `2+sqrt6` fails `-2+sqrt6`, and `negative 2`, `minus 2`, and `−2`/`–2` all equal `-2`. When both answers contain the same numbers in any order, each number's sign must also match: `3 + 5i` fails `5i - 3`, and `5, 7` fails `-5, 7`. A dash after a digit (`20 – 21`, `2 - 3i`) or attached to a letter (`G-2`, `A-1`) might be a range, subtraction, or label, so it isn't read as a sign. Leading zeros are kept (`001` ≠ `1`). The bare-number set comparison also skips multi-part keys, so a reversed answer to a "respectively" question (`18; 16` for `16; 18`) no longer passes.
- **A fraction is one number, not a list**: `1/2` standing by itself stays one word, so it never passes for a key of `1 AND 2`, nor `1 and 2` for a key of `1/2`. A slash inside a longer expression (`x^2/2`, `(a + b)/2`) is punctuation.
- **Part numbers aren't the parts**: for a key labelled `1) HALITE; 2) LISTRIC FAULT`, typing `1, 2` fails. A bare number only stands for a part that is a number once its label is off (`5, 7` for `1) 5 METERS; 2) 7 SECONDS`).

`npm test` checks these rules and grades every answer key and every whole-answer accept in the corpus against its own question (see [Local development and tests](#local-development-and-tests)).

```
tests/e2e/          browser tests (Playwright): Play, Multiplayer with simulated players, every page
.github/workflows/ci.yml   runs the syntax check and tests on every push and pull request
.github/workflows/deploy.yml  on every push to main, runs the unit tests and copies the site to sbreader/sbreader.github.io, which serves https://sbreader.github.io/
```

### `js/data.js` (`window.SBData`)
- **`SBData.loadMeta()`** fetches only `meta.json`; **`SBData.load()`** fetches it and then every tournament file in parallel, expanding each into the app's question shape (`expandTournament`) as it arrives, so that work overlaps the downloads still running. **`SBData.loadBookmarked()`** and **`SBData.loadIds(ids)`** do the same for just the tournaments holding the given questions (the Stats page uses `loadIds`). The catalog, solo and multiplayer pages include `data.js` in `<head>` and call `SBData.load()` there, so the downloads start before the rest of the page has loaded; `meta.json` is preloaded with `<link rel="preload">`. Expansion skips the text-cleanup regexes for the roughly 95% of strings that contain none of the characters they act on. `normalizeMeta` fixes up `meta` in one place so every page benefits:
  - `meta.subjects`, a flat array of keys, is expanded to `{key, label}` objects. This fixed a long-standing bug where subject chips rendered **with no text** on every page.
  - Subjects are reordered to **Math, Physics, Biology, Chemistry, Earth & Space, Energy**. (`meta.json` stores them alphabetically, a leftover of how the Python scripts built it.)
  - Question types are reordered so **Toss-up always comes before Bonus** (the label is "Toss-up").
- **Display-time text cleanup** (`cleanText` and friends). This is non-destructive, and each regex's scope was checked against the whole corpus:
  - `stripAuthorTag`: trailing Discord-style author tags (`name#1234`)
  - `stripTrailingWatermark`: trailing `2019 DSB ®`-style watermarks
  - `stripPronunciation`: bracketed phonetic hyphen-chains
  - `fixSpacedHyphens` / `fixSpacedApostrophes`: PDF-extraction spacing damage
- **`renderMathHTML`** turns real `^(...)`, `^token`, `_(...)`, and `_token` notation into `<sup>`/`<sub>`. Parenthesized groups may nest (`2^(2^(2^2))`, `n^(1/ln(n))`). By design it does **not** guess at lost notation; see [limitations](#known-limitations-and-open-items).
- **Chemical formulas**: after the math pass, `subscriptFormulas` writes the counts in formulas such as `H2SO4`, `Ca(OH)2` and `C6H12O6` as subscripts. A run counts as a formula only when every symbol is an element, no count is 1 or starts with 0, no element repeats back to back, and it isn't a lone element (except a short list of real molecules: `O2`, `N2`, `Cl2`, `S8`, `C60`, …) or one of the bank's look-alikes (`SN2`, `sp3`, `HSP70`, `IC50`, `H3K27`, `P700`, …). An ion written with its charge as a superscript (`H3^+`, `S2^(2-)`) gets its subscript even as a lone element. The stored text isn't changed, and grading ignores the subscripts.
- **`answerDisplayText(q)`**: for MC questions the choice text wins over `a.t`, so a damaged answer field still displays correctly.
- **`answerLineHTML()`**: one shared "Answer: X (also accept: Y)" renderer.
- **Question cards**: `questionCard` builds the Bootstrap card used in the catalog and on the stats and starred pages, from `appendQuestionBody`, `answerBlock` and `bookmarkButton`. `escapeHtml`, `QTYPE_LABELS` and `FORMAT_LABELS` are shared from here too.
- **Figures**: `questionFigures(q, opts)` builds the figure strip (lazy-loaded `<img>` with its width/height set so the layout doesn't shift, eager in the live question panel), `openLightbox(src, alt)` shows one full-size, and `preloadImages(q)` warms the next question's images. `expandQuestions` turns a record's `im` into `q.images` (`{src, w, h}`) and sets `q.visual`, which the filter and the tags read: `qtypeLabel(q)` / `qtypeTagClass(q)` give a visual bonus its "Visual Bonus" type tag in the cards and in the solo and multiplayer question panels.
- **`cleanRoundLabel()`** tidies round labels for display: it strips "Copy of", strips a repeated tournament name, and spaces letters from digits (`DE1` → `DE 1`).
- Also here: filtering (`filterQuestions`, including the `visuals` set: `visual`/`text`), shuffles (`shuffle`, `stratifiedShuffle`, `stratifiedQueue`), the dual round slider (`wireRoundRangeSlider`), the searchable multi-select (`enhanceMultiSelect`), range-fill styling, and starred-question storage (`SBData.bookmarks`, kept in memory, re-read when another tab changes it).

### Multiplayer networking
Peer-to-peer over WebRTC via PeerJS. The free public PeerJS broker is used only for the connection handshake; no game data goes through a server. Every player is connected to every other (a full mesh). The coordinator's browser rules on the game (queue, timers, buzzes, scoring) and broadcasts it; every player keeps a copy, so any of them carries on the moment it leaves (see **Rooms belong to nobody**). The other players send it intents (`join`, `buzz`, `submitAnswer`, `next`, `skip`, `togglePause`, `override`, `typing`, `updateSettings`, `ownerAction`, chat).

Anyone with the room's name can connect, so the coordinator passes every message through `SBMpRules.cleanHostMessage` first. Unknown types are dropped, strings are cut to length (name 24, answer 200, chat 300), filter values must exist in `meta.json` (a set name must be a known tournament, packet numbers only digits, commas and dashes), and the reading speed is clamped to 0–100. Peers that haven't sent `join` are ignored; the door answers `__hello` and `peek` and nothing else. Messages aren't rate-limited.

Connections use Google's public STUN servers, with no TURN relay; players who can't connect directly use the room's Supabase Realtime channel (see Relay above).

PeerJS only closes a connection once ICE reaches `failed`, which can take a long time after the other tab is closed. So `peer.js` also treats a connection whose ICE state stays `disconnected` for 3 s as gone (the browser's own consent checks drive that, unaffected by timer throttling). As a fallback, each side pings every 5 s and drops a connection that has been silent for 75 s, longer than a hidden tab's slowest timers. The pings stay inside `peer.js` and never reach the game code. A player whose page closes says goodbye (`__bye`) first, so the others take them off at once instead of waiting for these checks. A peer that loses the broker (but not its players) reconnects to it, so the door stays findable.

### Multiplayer relay (TURN)

Players connect directly when their networks allow it, and through the room's Supabase Realtime channel when they don't (see Relay under Multiplayer). A direct (WebRTC) link needs each browser to reach the other's address; STUN only tells a browser its public address, which isn't enough behind most phone networks (carrier-grade NAT), strict school or office firewalls, and symmetric NATs. A TURN server sits in between and forwards the encrypted traffic for those pairs. A TURN server is the standard, sturdier fix for networks that block direct connections, and the site uses one when it's set up: `supabase/functions/turn-credentials` is a Supabase Edge Function that turns a Cloudflare TURN key into logins that expire after a day, and `js/peer.js` asks it for them directly (not through the Supabase library) as soon as a connection is made, keeps them in `localStorage` for 12 hours, and waits for them at most 1.5 seconds before the first connection to another player. Without the function, nothing changes. Cloudflare's TURN service is free up to 1,000 GB a month.

Setting it up (once):
1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open **Realtime → TURN Server** (also listed as Calls → TURN) and create a TURN key. Note its **Key ID** and **API token** (shown once).
2. Install the Supabase CLI and link the project: `npx supabase login`, then `npx supabase link --project-ref <project-ref>` (the `<project-ref>` part of the project URL in `js/config.js`).
3. Store the key as the function's secrets: `npx supabase secrets set CLOUDFLARE_TURN_KEY_ID=<key id> CLOUDFLARE_TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN=<api token>`.
4. Deploy it: `npx supabase functions deploy turn-credentials --no-verify-jwt` (also set in `supabase/config.toml`). The site's publishable key isn't a JWT, so with JWT verification on, every request is refused. If you create the function in the dashboard instead, turn **Verify JWT with legacy secret** off in its settings.

Check it by opening `https://<project-ref>.supabase.co/functions/v1/turn-credentials` in a browser: it should answer `{"iceServers":[…]}` with `turn:` addresses. The key never reaches browsers; anyone who can load the site can get the short-lived logins, which is how TURN works on public sites. Cloudflare's dashboard shows the traffic used.

## Data format

### `data/bank/` (the question bank)
One JSON file per tournament, `data/bank/<slug>.json` (112 files, about 29 MB raw and 8.7 MB gzipped in all; GitHub Pages serves them gzipped and over HTTP/2, so they download in parallel). The catalog, solo and multiplayer pages load every file, because their filters and search span the whole bank; the Stats page loads only the ones it needs.

Each file is an array of **packets**. A packet holds the fields its questions share, and the questions themselves under `q`:

```json
[
{"rl":"AVES DE1","r":6,"u":"https://lebot.djiang.xyz/packets/AVES-2025/AVES%20DE1.pdf","q":[
{"i":0,"s":"math","f":"SA","qt":"tossup","n":1,"q":"Rohan is driving and …","a":{"t":"36"}},
{"i":1,"s":"math","f":"MC","qt":"bonus","n":1,"q":"How many positive integers …","a":{"t":"93","l":"Z"},"c":{"W":"50","X":"90","Y":"92","Z":"93"}}]},
…
]
```

Packet fields: `rl` (raw round label), `r` (round number, `null` if unlabeled) and `u` (packet URL, left out when there isn't one). A packet is a run of consecutive questions that share all three, so a tournament's questions keep their order. The tournament's name and slug aren't repeated on every question: they come from `meta.json`. Leaving those five repeated fields off each question is what makes the bank about a third smaller than the old single `data/questions.json`. Each line holds one question, so a change to a question shows up as a one-line diff.

`data/bank/index.json` is `{"ids": [[firstId, slug], …]}`: runs of consecutive ids, in id order, that belong to the same tournament (about 430 runs). `SBData.slugForId` finds a question's tournament with a binary search.

**Editing the bank.** `tools/bank.js` converts between these files and the flat form, one record per question carrying every field (the table below), which is what the old `data/questions.json` held:

```bash
node tools/bank.js unpack /tmp/questions.json   # bank -> flat list, in meta.json's tournament order
# ...edit or audit /tmp/questions.json...
node tools/bank.js pack /tmp/questions.json     # flat list -> data/bank/ (rewrites every file and index.json)
npm test
```

`pack` groups consecutive questions of a tournament into packets, writes one file per tournament slug, deletes the file of any tournament that no longer has questions, and rebuilds `index.json`. It doesn't touch `meta.json`, so update its counts (and add a new tournament's entry, with its `level`) when they change; `npm test` fails until the two agree, and also checks that every file is exactly what `pack` would write. Small edits can also be made to a tournament's file directly.

**Checking new questions against their packets.** After adding questions, put their packets' text in a folder (`pdftotext`, a Google Doc's `?format=txt` export, a .docx's text), with a `urls.json` that maps each packet's URL to its file, and run:

```bash
node tools/subject-audit.js /tmp/packets --write
```

It finds each new question's stem in the packets and reads the header printed before it. It prints every question filed under a different subject than the label there, and every question whose type or number differs from the `TOSS-UP 5)` / `BONUS 5)` marker; it records the printed subjects in `data/source-subjects.json` (`null` when it can't find the stem or its label). Read each difference. For a subject, fix the question when the packet is right, or add `"<id>": "printed→filed"` to `data/subject-overrides.json` when the packet mislabels it. For a type or number, fix the question when the packet is right; packets misprint headers too (`TOSS-UP 19` twice, `BONUS 8` right after tossup 18), so check the questions around it first. Type and number are read only from a question's own packet (listed in `urls.json`), since a copy elsewhere can sit in another slot. `--all` re-checks questions that already have an entry, without changing them.

A flat record (a question plus its packet's fields and its tournament):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `i` | unique integer id (the only hard uniqueness constraint) |
| `t` / `ts` | tournament name / slug |
| `r` | round number, or `null` for unlabeled |
| `rl` | raw round label (e.g. `RR 5`, `Set 3 · Round 12`) |
| `s` | subject key: `math`, `phys`, `bio`, `chem`, `ess`, `energy` |
| `f` | format: `SA` or `MC` |
| `qt` | `tossup` or `bonus` |
| `n` | question number within the round |
| `q` | question text |
| `c` | MC choices `{W, X, Y, Z}` |
| `a` | answer: `t` text, `l` MC letter, `ac` also-accept list, `rj` do-not-accept list |
| `u` | source packet URL, always `https://` (on 93,463 records) |
| `im` | figures for a visual question, in order: `[{f, w, h}]`, `f` a path under `data/img/` (`mit-2025/17415.webp`), `w`/`h` its pixel size. Absent on text-only questions. |
| `pg` | where the question is in its packet: the page of a PDF, or the line of a GitHub text file. The site adds it to the packet link as `#page=N` or `#LN`, so a question's source link opens the packet there (browsers' built-in PDF viewers honour `#page=`; Safari on iOS opens page 1). A set's or packet's own **Source packet** link, on its page, is to the whole packet, with no page or line. Found by `tools/packet-locations.js`; absent when the packet has no pages or lines to point at (Google Docs and Drive, isobowl, scibowldb, GitHub `.doc`/`.docx`/`.pdf`) or the question couldn't be found in it. |

`(rl, n, qt)` is **not** guaranteed unique. Some packets really do contain spare or replacement questions that reuse a number (e.g. NWI 2025 DE5, CLASH "21A/21B", FE!M 2025).

### `data/meta.json`
`totalQuestions`, `tournaments[{slug, name, count, level}]` (sorted case-insensitively by name), `subjects`, `rounds`, `formats`, `qtypes`, `levels`, `idAliases`, `generatedAt`. It's rebuilt from the bank after every data change, and `totalQuestions` always equals both the sum of tournament counts and the number of questions in the bank. Every tournament listed has a file in `data/bank/`, and vice versa.

Each tournament also has a `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, approximate where the event's day isn't known) used for the catalog's newest-first order; a new tournament needs one.

`level` is `hs` or `ms` and belongs to the tournament, not the record: every question inherits its tournament's level at load time (`SBData.load()`), which drives the Level filter chips (the level isn't shown as a tag on questions). The middle-school tournaments are NSB MS Regs, CLASH 2026 MS, Bay Ultimate MS Scibowl, BUMS 2026, Dasoni Standard1, Deadbird Invitational, LOST 3 MS, MIT MS Study Guides and MOOSE 2021 (14,231 questions); everything else is `hs` (89,409). **A rebuild of `meta.json` has to carry each tournament's `level` over**, and a new tournament needs one assigned (a missing `level` is treated as `hs`).

`idAliases` maps the id of every question removed as a duplicate to the id of the copy that was kept; `SBData.load()` uses it to move saved bookmarks onto the kept copy, so merging duplicates never silently deletes someone's star. Keep it when rebuilding `meta.json`. One known blur: "Random Stuff" is a grab-bag tournament that includes one MS earth-science packet but is tagged `hs` as a whole.

> **Visual bonuses are implemented, and every scrape must collect them.** A question that comes with a figure (a visual bonus, or the occasional visual tossup) is stored with its image and shown in the catalog, solo and multiplayer modes, with its own Visual filter. When packets are scraped, their visual questions are scraped too, figure and all, from the packet itself or from the event's separate visual-bonus deck. They are never skipped as "visual"; one is left out only when its image exists nowhere that can be reached. See [Visual questions](#visual-questions).
>
> **Every question from every tournament and packet is included.** Questions with similar wording, or that test the same thing as another question, are allowed and kept, in the same tournament or across tournaments. A question an event prints more than once (in two rounds, twice in one round, or in both its HS and MS divisions) stays every time. The only questions dropped as duplicates are **mass copies**: the word-for-word copies inside a packet that is itself mostly copies of another packet (a packet re-posted under another name, or the same file ingested twice), and the same question parsed twice from one slot. When in doubt, keep. Apart from that, a question is left out only when it can't be used: no answer, text garbled past repair, a figure that exists nowhere, or a fact that is no longer true. See [Inclusion and duplicate policy](#inclusion-and-duplicate-policy).
>
> **Every question is stored the way the site shows it.** Pronunciation guides, moderator prompts and writer notes are removed, answer keys hold only the answer (alternates go to the accept and reject lists), spoken math is written in notation, and math uses `^`/`_` markup. The same rules apply to every packet, old or newly scraped. See [Text and notation conventions](#text-and-notation-conventions).

## Visual questions

Some bonuses (and a few tossups) are about a picture: a diagram, graph, micrograph, spectrum, structure or photo. **Visual questions are fully implemented**: the record carries its figure, every page shows it, and a filter selects them. Very early ingests skipped them because there was nowhere to put an image; that is no longer a reason to skip anything.

**Scraping rule: always scrape visual bonuses.** When a packet is ingested, its visual questions go in with their figures like any other question. Look for the figure in the packet first (embedded images; vector drawings rendered from the page), then in the event's visual-bonus deck (slides, a separate PDF, a Google Slides export, speaker notes), then in the structured source (isobowl's `imageUrl`). Only when the image exists nowhere reachable is the question left out, or kept as text if it can be answered without the picture, and the gap goes under [Known limitations](#known-limitations-and-open-items).

- **Visual sweep (October 2026)**: all 1,976 packet files were searched for visual markers ("Visual Bonus" headers, NSBA1's "VISHWAL BONUS", "shown in the image", "pictured", "depicted", "the following figure/graph/pedigree", VB slide titles) and every hit was matched against the bank. All but a handful already carry their figure. Added: ICSBT 2's DE7 Brassicaceae bonus (its floral diagram is embedded in the .docx) and its ext4 "visual" bonus, which has no picture and is answerable as text. Two text-only copies that sat in a tossup slot beside the real visual bonus (NSBA1 Playoffs 2.2's Vish Fish, Finals 3's Eulerian path) and one in LOST 1 Round 13 (Bullet Cluster) were removed, with `idAliases` pointing at the visual record. Still without a figure, because none was published: Texas 2026's three visual bonuses (Round 12's sandstones and Round 13's beach profiles are left out; Round 13's 7-segment display is in the bank as text, since the display is standard), National Chemistry Bowl DE6's three (the Google Doc says "shown above" but holds no image), Fall 2023 Round 14's pipe-flow bonus (its .docx image is an empty placeholder), and SBST's and SBST (Spring)'s visual rounds (their packets never included the figures).

- **Which questions**: 535 records in 31 tournaments carry an `im` list (541 images; NSBA1's and Bash 2025's two-part figures have two images each, and one WSBT bonus has three). The largest sets are LOST 2 (46), SBL (44), MIT 2025 (36), LOST 1 (34), NSBA1 (31), ESBOT 2 and CLASH 2026 HS (30 each), DASONI 2 and HSBT 2026 (27 each), Bash 2025 and MIT 2024 (25 each), WSBT (23) and Fall 2023 (22). 99 of them were already in the bank as text and got their figure back; the rest were added. Where an existing record's text had been cut short, it was replaced with the packet's full text. Per-tournament counts are in [docs/tournaments.md](docs/tournaments.md), and how they were found and checked is in [docs/data-history.md](docs/data-history.md#visual-questions).
- **Where the images come from**: images embedded in the PDF and .docx packets of the user's packet archive (matched to the bank by question text and cropped to the figure; where a packet's image runs off the page, the embedded picture itself was taken instead of a page render); the separate visual-bonus slide decks (MIT 2024/2025, Fall 2023, DAST, CLASH, FE!M, …), rendered slide by slide with LibreOffice and trimmed to the figure; LOST 1's and LOST 2's slide decks from scibowl.live; HSBT 2026's public visual-bonus deck (Google Slides, exported as PDF); figures drawn inline in SBL's packets and in ICSBT 3's public PDFs; the public SMH 2026 Google Docs; and isobowl's structured Trio Online Math Bowl rounds. The card's `width`/`height` attributes reserve the space before the image arrives.

- **Grading** is unchanged: the answer still comes from the record's key and accept list.

## Inclusion and duplicate policy

Each set has a `kind` in `meta.json`, shown on the Set List: a **tournament** (a competition's packets, or a team's practice rounds in that format), a **practice set** (a team's or community's question collection with no rounds behind it: Mira Loma Question Database, Stuyvesant Question Bank, Tycho Practice Rounds, Evan Zhong Study Sets, MIT MS Study Guides, ScibowlDB HW, 2016 Question Exchange, Random, Random Stuff, Summer 2019, David Rounds 2019, CSUN Regional Practice Rounds) or **another competition** in the same format (National Ocean Sciences Bowl, the National Chemistry Bowl sets, Lexington Biology Bowl, Trio Online Math Bowl).

**What's kept.** Every question of every tournament packet, with these in particular:

- **Similar questions stay.** Two questions that are worded alike, ask about the same fact, or test the same idea are both kept, whether they sit in one tournament or in two. So are reworded versions of an older question (an SA turned into an MC, a "which of the following" turned into "identify all"), and a lone question one event borrowed word for word from another.
- **Repeats inside one event stay.** A question printed in two rounds of the same tournament, twice in one round, or in both the HS and MS divisions of an event is kept every time it appears, under its own round and number.

**What's left out.** A question is left out for one of these reasons and no other; every scrape and every cleanup pass applies all of them.

1. **It can't be used**: no answer in the packet, text garbled past repair even with the page image (a confident guess counts as a repair: see below), a figure that was never published, or an answer key that is wrong and can't be worked out. Wrong keys that can be worked out are corrected instead.
2. **Its answer depends on when it was written** (any set): what is "currently" the largest, newest or most recent, how many of something are known, a count or share that has since changed (112 elements, Neptune's 13 moons, "coal produces the highest percentage of our electricity"), a mission still in the future when the packet was printed. A question stays when the fact still holds or it carries its own date in a way that stays true ("as of 1991, the most recent …").
3. **Its key is not something a player can type**: an explanatory sentence (see Text and notation conventions).
4. **It is a mass duplicate** (any set): word for word the same as another question (ignoring case, spacing, punctuation, list markers, pronunciation guides and author tags, but never math signs or numbers) **and** in a packet that is mostly copies too: at least half of that packet's questions are word-for-word copies of another packet, which means it is the same packet posted again under another name or ingested twice. The same question parsed twice from one slot, and a damaged copy of a question printed beside its clean original, count as copies too.
5. **It is a copy in a practice set**: a practice-set question that is word for word the same as a tournament question (the tournament copy is kept) or as another practice-set question (the earliest is kept). The test is the same as in 4, with the choices compared too. `npm test` fails if one comes back.
6. **It is geography or minor history, not science** (any set, tournament packets included). *Geography*: a place name or a ranking of places as the answer or the point of the question: the highest mountain, the deepest lake, the largest desert, which ocean or country holds something, which canal crosses which isthmus, which state or country produces or uses the most of something. Kept: plate boundaries and the features they make, features of other planets and moons, and the science of oceans, weather and climate even when a place is named (El Niño, lake-effect snow, rain-shadow deserts). *Minor history*: spaceflight firsts, missions and launch dates (who walked on the Moon, which rover launched in which year), "in what year" trivia, inventors of everyday devices, and prize or biography trivia. Kept: who made or proposed a major piece of science, and the experiment or model itself (Wegener and continental drift, Darwin, Mendel, Pasteur and Koch, Michelson-Morley, Millikan, Rutherford, the atomic models of Dalton, Thomson and Bohr, Mendeleev, Copernicus, Galileo, Hubble), a Nobel question whose answer is the science, and the history of computing.
7. **It isn't science at all** (practice sets and other competitions only): laws, treaties and agencies, who operates a ship, and the like. Computer science is science and stays, as do instruments.
8. **It is a joke round** (SMH 2025's boss battle, Bay Ultimate's BUMS Special). Themed rounds and single joke-framed questions inside regular rounds are kept.

Every removed copy (4 and 5) is recorded in `meta.json`'s `idAliases`, so a star on it moves to the kept copy. A star on a question removed for another reason is dropped.

## Text and notation conventions

Questions are read on screen, not heard, so the bank stores each one the way a player should see it. These rules were set by earlier cleanup passes (the history is in [docs/data-history.md](docs/data-history.md)). They apply to every packet, and every new scrape has to follow them too.

**Remove text meant for the moderator or the writer**
- Pronunciation guides (`[my-OH-sis]`, `(pron: …)`, `Voigt (voyt)`) and spoken readings of math or formulas (`0.3939... [zero point three nine three nine repeating]`, `(read as: …)`) are removed. Where a reading was the only copy of the math, the math is written out instead.
- Moderator prompts and reading aids go too: the 1991–93 sets' "Is it:" before the choices, spelled-out words and acronyms ("spelled A-B-R-A", "G-I-G-O"), "(short pause)", "Read slowly".
- So do writer tags and drafting notes (`[JR]`, `name#1234`, "hi please write some phys or chem"), packet section headers glued to stems ("(Mech) – Multiple Choice"), page footers and watermarks, and template slots ("Your bonus question goes here.").

**Answer keys hold the answer and nothing else**
- Inline `(ACCEPT: …)`, `ACCEPT …` and `(DO NOT ACCEPT: …)` notes are moved into the accept (`ac`) and reject (`rj`) lists. Moderator notes such as "(must give units)" or "(order irrelevant)" are dropped. A note that changes grading is kept as grading data: "do not accept" parts go to the reject list, "accept either" parts to the accept list, and "must give both" keys join their parts with AND.
- On a multi-part key split by `;`, an accept entry may be an alternate for one part (`DIAMONDOIDS` for `1) ADAMANTANES; …`), and the grader matches it to that part. On any other key, every accept entry is graded as a whole answer. An alternate for one item of a list such as `OXYGEN AND WATER` is therefore written as a whole answer; a lone `O2` would pass by itself. A list of substances gets one accept in formulas, in key order (`O2 AND H2O`), and the grader mixes names and formulas from it. Gases also get the bare symbols (`CO2 AND N`, so `co2 n` passes), and ions both the charged and bare forms (`Na^+ AND K^+`, `Na AND K`). A question that asks for elements gets just their symbols (`Fe AND Ni`). For the same reason, an accept list that a scrape split at its commas (`S0`, `SBa`, `Sc` for a ranking keyed `1, 2, 3`) is joined back into one entry (`S0, SBa, Sc`).
- Accept entries that say nothing specific ("EQUIVALENTS", "WORD FORMS", "or similar", "ANYTHING THAT SOUNDS VAGUELY SIMILAR") are dropped. Entries that describe a range or class ("ANYTHING BETWEEN 90 AND 95", "Anything metallic") stay, for the player who overrides.
- An identify-all or ranking key is written `1, 3, 2`, not `132`.
- A multiple-choice answer's text is exactly the keyed choice's text. A question printed as short answer with W–Z choices in its stem is stored as multiple choice (multi-part bonuses with a choice set inside one part stay short answer).
- **Four different choices**: a choice set with a full stop after some choices and not others has them removed. A choice printed twice is rebuilt only when the other three and the key leave one reading (a 2 × 2 of two properties with one cell missing); otherwise it stays, or the question goes if the keyed answer is the repeated one.
- **A multiple-choice question has no accept list**. Multiple choice is graded by the letter or the exact choice text and nothing else, so an "also accept" on its card promised an answer that would be marked wrong.
- **An accept or reject entry is an answer, never a sentence about answers**. No leading "or" / "and" (`or lurch` → `lurch`), no "must specify …", "either answer", "equivalent forms", "and any variation"; a "do not accept" filed under accept moves to the reject list; an entry that repeats the key or another entry is dropped. Two kinds of note stay because the grader or the player uses them: an order note ("in any order", "in that order"), and a range or class the player can override on ("ANYTHING BETWEEN 90 AND 95").
- **Alternates go in the accept list, not in the key**: a key printed `A or B`, `A/B` (two names for one thing) or `A (or B)` is stored as `A` with `B` accepted, and an accept entry holds one alternate, not `B or C`. Left as printed: an "or" that joins the two halves of one answer (`x = 0 or x = 9` becomes `x = 0 AND x = 9` when both are required), "one of …" keys, lists, and a slash that belongs to the answer.
- **A key is something a player can type**. A question whose answer is an explanatory sentence ("Why …?", "What does … mean?", keyed with a sentence of nine words or more) is left out of the bank, or its key is cut down to the fact asked for when there is one (`THE WOMAN IS 53 YEARS OLD, THE DAUGHTER IS 31 YEARS OLD` → `53 AND 31`).
- **No notes in brackets in a key**: an order note moves to the accept list as `in any order` / `in that order` (the grader reads those), `(only)`, `(ALL)`, `(or equivalent)`, `(Solution = …)`, `(answer must be negative)` are dropped, and `(NOT 56)` becomes a reject. A bracket that is part of the answer stays (`ATP (ADENOSINE TRIPHOSPHATE)`), as do the "name any N" forms (`(ANY TWO) …`, `… (any 3)`).
- **The key starts with the answer**: no "ANSWER:" in front, no choice letter on a short-answer key (`Y) ECCENTRICITY` → `ECCENTRICITY`), no bracket left open by a note that was cut away, and no full stop after a short key (`CARBONIC.` → `CARBONIC`; a key that is a whole sentence keeps its own).

**Write math in notation**
- Spoken math becomes notation: "8 TIMES THE SQUARE ROOT OF 14" becomes `8√14`, "4PI" becomes `4π`, "left parenthesis x + 1 right parenthesis" becomes `(x + 1)`, `sqrt(…)` becomes `√…`, and `*` or `x` used as a times sign before `10^` becomes `×`. In answer keys, the spoken form stays as an accepted answer. A spoken key whose grouping is ambiguous ("1 + ROOT 2 OVER 2") is left as printed.
- Exponents and subscripts use `^` and `_` markup, never Unicode super- or subscript characters: `x^2`, `cm^-1`, `^(13)C`, `log_2 x`, `∫_0^1`. A bare exponent ends where its leading digits end (`x^3y`), so a longer exponent needs parentheses (`e^(-2t)`). Chemical formulas are written with plain digits (`H2SO4`, `[Fe(CN)6]^(3-)`), and the site renders the subscripts.
- Ion charges are superscripts: `Ca^(2+)`, `NO3^-`, `H^+`. So are hybridizations (`sp^3d^2`) and electron configurations (`1s^2 2s^2 2p^6`).
- A negative number has no space after its sign (`-2`, `–4`). The display joins `X - Y` into `X-Y`, so "is - 2" would show as "is-2".
- **One character for one symbol**, so that a search or a typed answer finds it and it looks the same everywhere: `Δ` (Greek delta, not the look-alike `∆`), `μ` (not the micro sign `µ`), `ε`, `φ`, `α` (not `ϵ`, `ϕ`, `ɑ`), `°` for degrees (not `˚`, `º`, `◦`, `ᵒ`) and `°C` (not the single character `℃`), `·` for a multiplication dot (not `⋅`, `∙`, `•`), `≤` `≥` (not `≦` `≧`), a plain hyphen (not the no-break hyphen), accented letters as one character (`ö`, not `o` + a loose `¨`), and no invisible characters. A times sign before a power of ten is `×` whatever the packet used (`3 ∗ 10^5`, `6.9⋅10^-3`, `9 • 10^9` → `3 × 10^5` …).
- One-character fractions (`½`, `¾`, `⅔`) may stay as printed: the grader reads them as `1/2`, `3/4`, `2/3`, and a mixed number as `2 1/2`. Greek letters stay as letters; the grader takes their names (`omega` for `ω`, `delta H` for `ΔH`).
- Unit names are lower case with their prefix (`microfarads`, `kilopascals`, `centimorgans`, not `microFarads`), and a function is written `f(x)`, not `f (x)` or `f ( x )`.
- A question starts with a capital letter, unless it starts with a name written small (`p53`, `tRNA`, `pH`).
- There's no space before `?`, `,` or a closing `:`, and none inside `√3`, `10^-3` or `σ/3`. Compound names split by a spaced dash ("MICHAELIS – MENTEN") are hyphenated. Compound words split by a spaced hyphen ("light - dependent", "Bose - Einstein", "2 - cyclohexen - 1 - ol") are joined too. A dash that separates a label from its description ("Pearl – Calcite", "I – Mitochondrial Matrix", "TSH – thyroid gland") is an en dash with spaces, because the display joins any "X - Y" written with a plain hyphen. A minus sign keeps its spaces. Math-italic letters (𝑥, 𝜋) are plain letters.

**Fix extraction damage, not content**
- Misspellings, doubled words and split or glued words are corrected (recieve, Heisenburg, Avagadro, "a a result").
- **Confident guesses are allowed; fabrication isn't.** Damaged or missing text may be filled in when exactly one reading fits the context, the math and the answer key, and the guess is recorded in [docs/data-history.md](docs/data-history.md#confident-guess-rule). When more than one reading stays plausible, or the guess would decide the answer, the text is left as printed or the question is left out.
- A formatting pass changes formatting only. A wrong answer key is corrected only when the right answer can be worked out, and every correction is recorded in [Wrong-key corrections](docs/data-history.md#wrong-key-corrections).

**Placement**
- A tournament's rounds are numbered in order through its phases (round robin, then double elimination or playoffs, then finals), so the round slider reads in order.
- **Energy is a last resort.** Every question filed under Energy, whatever its packet called it (NSB's Energy category, General Science, Computer Science, special rounds), is read. If it squarely belongs to Math, Physics, Biology, Chemistry or Earth & Space, it moves there. The question is judged by what it asks, not by a "Researchers at X National Lab" lead-in. Energy keeps computer science, energy technology and resources (reactors, grids, batteries, fuels, power statistics and policy), units and lab practice, history-of-science trivia, and questions that need two subjects about equally. When in doubt, the question stays in Energy. Every new scrape has to apply this too. A single-subject event (Earth and Space Scrimmage, the National Chemistry Bowl, Lexington Biology Bowl, Trio Online Math Bowl) keeps every question in its subject.
- **A question's subject matches what its packet prints**, unless the packet mislabels it. `data/source-subjects.json` records the printed subject of every question (`"other"` for Energy, General Science, Physical Science and the like, which the Energy rule places; `null` where no packet could be checked), and `npm test` fails when a question's subject differs from it without an entry in `data/subject-overrides.json`. An override is written `"printed→filed"`, so changing that question's subject again makes the test fail until someone re-reads it. A packet's label is overridden only after reading the question, when it belongs to the other subject at least as much (an enzyme question printed under Chemistry, a Kepler's-laws question under Physics); a question that plainly fits the printed subject keeps it. Every new scrape records the printed subjects with `tools/subject-audit.js` (see [Editing the bank](#databank-the-question-bank)) and settles each difference it lists. A single-subject event's questions are all recorded as its subject.
- Every tournament has a `level` and an approximate `date` in `meta.json` (see [Data format](#datametajson)).

## Accounts

Accounts are optional and off until a Supabase project is set up; without one everything else works (stars and settings stay in the browser), and the navbar's "Log in" opens a page saying accounts aren't switched on. They use [Supabase](https://supabase.com) for sign-up/login (its Auth, email + password) and a small database, called straight from the browser with supabase-js 2.115.0, loaded from the jsDelivr CDN (with an integrity hash) only when accounts are switched on.

**Setting it up (once):**
1. Create a free project at supabase.com.
2. In its SQL Editor, run `supabase/schema.sql`. It creates three account tables (`bookmarks`, `answers`, `user_settings`) with row-level security, so each account can only read and change its own rows, and the two multiplayer registry tables (`mp_rooms`, `mp_players`), which hold only room names, player names and practice scores and are what the Multiplayer page lists public rooms and player counts from. Nobody reads or writes those two directly: the site calls functions (`mp_list`, which lists public rooms only; `mp_roster`, a room's players by its exact name; `mp_heartbeat`, which saves this browser's own row, and deletes the players and rooms that have lapsed (see Room list and player counts under Multiplayer); `mp_room`, a room's owner key, bans, controlled switch and players in one call; `mp_set_room`, with which a room's creator records those). Each browser keeps a random secret (`localStorage` `sb_mp_secret`); a player row stores a hash of it and only that browser can change the row, and only a room's creator can make it public or private. Projects set up before November 8, 2026 should run the file again to switch this on; until then the site uses the tables directly, as before.
3. Under Authentication → URL Configuration, set the Site URL to the site's address (`https://sbreader.github.io/`) and add `…/account.html` to the redirect URLs (used by the confirmation and password-reset emails).
4. Put the Project URL (the **Connect** button at the top of the dashboard, or Project Settings → Data API; it looks like `https://<project-ref>.supabase.co`) and the publishable key (Project Settings → API Keys, `sb_publishable_…`; a legacy anon key works too) in `js/config.js`. Both are meant to be public; the row-level security is what protects the data. Never use the secret key.

**What an account keeps** (`js/account.js`, with the network-free parts in `js/account-core.js`):
- **Starred questions**: every star is mirrored to the account (the table is still called `bookmarks`). At login, this browser's stars and the account's are merged (a union, so nothing is lost on either side). The account's stars are read in pages (Supabase returns at most 1,000 rows a request), and the merge runs only on the complete list, so a cut-off read is never taken for stars deleted elsewhere.
- **Answers**: each question answered in Play (`solo`) or Multiplayer (`mp`, only your own buzzes; a question you didn't buzz on counts as skipped), with its subject, type, format and tournament, the points it scored (4, 0 or −4) and its celerity, for the stats and graphs pages. A grading override updates the answer just recorded. (Points and celerity were added on November 10, 2026: **run `supabase/schema.sql` again** on a project set up before then to add the two columns. Until that's done answers are still saved, without them, and count as 4 for a right answer and 0 otherwise, with no celerity; so do answers recorded before the change.)
- **Settings**: theme, reading speed, multiplayer name and "Hide answers". At login the newer copy wins; after that, changes are pushed as they happen.

Nothing else is stored with the account, and nothing of it is shared with other players. "Delete my data" on the Account page removes the account's rows (stars in the browser stay). The multiplayer registry isn't tied to accounts: it holds the name you play under in each room and your score there.

### Reports and "I was right"

Every question card (Database results, the Play and Multiplayer history, packet pages) has a **Report** link: a short form (the answer is wrong, the question is garbled, a figure is missing, the subject or source is wrong, or something else, plus an optional note) that saves a row to `question_reports`. Separately, whenever a player overrides a wrong grade to correct, what they typed and the key go to `answer_overrides`: an answer that keeps appearing there belongs in the question's accept list. Both work logged in or out (`js/reports.js`); anyone can add a row and nobody can read them through the site.

To switch them on, run `supabase/schema.sql` again (it's safe to re-run; the two tables are near the end). Then read them in the dashboard's Table Editor, or in the SQL Editor:

```sql
-- Newest reports, with how many each question has had
select question_id, reason, note, page, created_at,
       count(*) over (partition by question_id) as reports_for_question
from question_reports order by created_at desc limit 100;

-- Answers players most often overrode to correct
select question_id, lower(typed) as typed, answer_key, count(*) as times
from answer_overrides group by 1, 2, 3 order by times desc limit 100;
```

To fix a reported question, find its tournament from its id in `data/bank/index.json` (or search its text in the Database), correct it in `data/bank/` (see [Data format](#data-format)), and delete the rows once dealt with.

### Question stats

As on QB Reader, each Database card's **Stats** link shows how everyone has done on that question. Every play is recorded, logged in or not: each question in Play once its result is final (when the next question starts, or the page is left, so an "I was wrong" / "I was right" counts), and in Multiplayer your own buzzes, plus a question read in full that nobody got and you didn't buzz on. A question skipped before it was read through isn't counted. A play is a row in `question_plays` (question, toss-up or bonus, `correct` / `incorrect` / `neg` / `unanswered`, the celerity of a right answer, Play or Multiplayer, the time; no account or name). Nobody reads or writes the table directly: the site calls `question_play` to add a row and `question_stats` to sum one question's rows, both plain REST calls (`js/question-stats.js`), so they work without supabase-js.

To switch them on, run `supabase/schema.sql` again (the table and the two functions are at the end). Until then the Stats window says question stats aren't set up yet.

## Deploying

The official site is **https://sbreader.github.io/**. It used to be `https://unweidlykhan349.github.io/sbreader/`.

The code lives and is worked on here, in `UnweidlyKhan349/sbreader`. A `<name>.github.io` address can only be served by a repo of the account called `<name>`, so the site itself is served from a copy, the `sbreader` organization's `sbreader.github.io` repo:

```
UnweidlyKhan349/sbreader      ← push here
        │  .github/workflows/deploy.yml, on every push to main
        ▼
sbreader/sbreader.github.io   ← copy only, served at https://sbreader.github.io/
```

The workflow runs the unit tests (not the browser tests, which run separately in CI and don't hold up a deploy), copies the site (everything but `.git`, `.github`, `tests`, `tools` and `supabase`) and pushes it to the copy's `main` branch, replacing what was there; the copy serves it with **Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch**, `main`, `/ (root)`. Never edit the copy by hand: the next deploy overwrites it. It can also be run by hand from the Actions tab (**Deploy to sbreader.github.io → Run workflow**).

Setting it up (done once; listed in case it has to be redone):
1. Create the `sbreader` organization (Free plan) and in it an empty **public** repo named `sbreader.github.io`.
2. Make a deploy key: `ssh-keygen -t ed25519 -N "" -C "sbreader deploy" -f sbreader_deploy`. Add `sbreader_deploy.pub` to `sbreader/sbreader.github.io` under Settings → Deploy keys, with **Allow write access**. Add the private key `sbreader_deploy` to `UnweidlyKhan349/sbreader` under Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions, named `SBREADER_DEPLOY_KEY`. Then delete both files.
3. After the first deploy, turn on Pages in `sbreader/sbreader.github.io` (Deploy from a branch, `main`, `/ (root)`).

The site itself has no build step, `npm install`, or environment variables; the deploy adds the set and packet pages (below). `package.json` only defines the test commands and has no dependencies.

The site sits at the domain root, so `404.html` (served for any missing address, at any depth) uses root-relative links (`/css/style.css`, `/solo`). Every other page uses relative ones and would work under any path.

### The set and packet pages

The deploy also runs `tools/build-pages.js`, which writes a real HTML page for every set (`/sets/<slug>/`) and every packet (`/sets/<slug>/<n>`), about 2,600 in all, with the questions and answers in the HTML, so search engines can find individual tournaments and questions. Each is `set.html` / `packet.html` with the content filled in, a `<base href="/">` and `data-set` / `data-packet` on `#setPage`; `js/set-pages.js` then redraws it in the browser with the interactive parts (downloads, Report links, enlarging figures). The dynamic `set?set=…` and `packet?set=…&packet=…` pages still work, and name the static page as their canonical address. The deploy also replaces `sitemap.xml` with one that adds every set and packet. The pages aren't in the repository: build them locally with `node tools/build-pages.js <folder>` after copying the site there.

### Caching (`sw.js`)

A service worker (`sw.js`, registered by `js/site.js`) keeps the site's files in the browser, so a repeat visit doesn't download the 29 MB bank again and Play works offline once it has loaded:
- A file whose address has a content stamp (`?v=…`) can't change at that address, so it's served from the cache. That covers the scripts and stylesheets (stamped by `tools/stamp-assets.js`) and every bank file: `meta.json` holds each file's stamp (`v` per tournament, and `bankIndex`), written by `tools/meta-counts.js`, and the site fetches `data/bank/<slug>.json?v=<stamp>`. A new stamp replaces the old copy.
- Pages, `meta.json`, `version.json` and figures are fetched from the network first (revalidated with the server, so the browser's own 10-minute cache can't return a page from before a deploy), with the cache only as the offline fallback.
- Other sites (Supabase, PeerJS) aren't touched.

After any change to `data/bank/`, run `node tools/meta-counts.js` (as before); `npm test` fails if a stamp is out of date. To switch the worker off for everyone, replace `sw.js` with one that unregisters itself and deletes its caches.

### Updates after a redeploy

Every deploy runs `tools/stamp-version.js`, which writes `version.json` (the commit's SHA) and puts the same value in every page as `<meta name="sb-version">`. `js/site.js` fetches `version.json` (never from a cache) when a page loads, when its tab comes back into view, and every five minutes. If the site is newer than the page, nothing is shown and the page is not reloaded: it carries on as it is, and you get the new version when you refresh or next open the site. The only thing done meanwhile is having the service worker fetch its own new copy, so that refresh gets the new files. A copy served without the meta tag (local development) never checks.

### Search engines and link previews

- Every indexable page has a `<meta name="description">`, a `<link rel="canonical">`, and Open Graph/Twitter tags (title and summary; there's no preview image), so shared links get a title and summary.
- **Addresses have no `.html`**: every link is `solo`, `catalog?tournament=…`, `set?set=…` and so on, and the home page is `./`. GitHub Pages serves `/solo` from `solo.html`, so both forms work; the navbar highlights the current page either way. The one exception is the account emails, which send people to `account.html`, the address in the Supabase project's redirect allowlist.
- **Site name**: the home page carries `WebSite` structured data (JSON-LD) naming the site **SB Reader** (alternates "SBReader", as the page titles and the logo write it, "Science Bowl Reader" and "sbreader.github.io"); `og:site_name`, the web app manifest and the page's own text use the same name. Google shows a site name above each result's title and takes it from the home page of the domain *or subdomain*; a subdomain whose home page doesn't state one gets the parent domain's, which for `github.io` is "GitHub Pages documentation". Google picks the change up when it next crawls the home page (days to weeks); **URL Inspection → Request indexing** for `https://sbreader.github.io/` in Search Console asks for that sooner.
- `account.html`, `stats.html` and `404.html` are `noindex`: there's nothing on them to index.
- `sitemap.xml` lists the public pages (without `.html`); the deployed copy also lists every set and packet page (see above). `robots.txt` allows everything and names the sitemap; the site is at the domain root, so crawlers read it. The sitemap can also be submitted, as `https://sbreader.github.io/sitemap.xml`, in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- The canonical, Open Graph, sitemap and `robots.txt` URLs name `https://sbreader.github.io/`, and `404.html` links from the domain root. A fork or a move to another address needs them changed (`grep -rn "sbreader.github.io" *.html sitemap.xml robots.txt`); hosting under a path such as `/<repo>/` also needs `404.html`'s links prefixed with it.

## Local development and tests

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000/

npm test        # or: node --test tests/*.test.js  (Node 18+, no install needed)
```

The tests, which CI also runs on every push and pull request:
- `tests/answer-check.test.js`: grading rules, the known false positives, and every answer key and whole-answer accept in the corpus graded against its own question.
- `tests/mp-rules.test.js`: multiplayer message validation and override rules.
- `tests/data.test.js`: every record has the fields and values the site expects, ids are unique, `meta.json` counts match the bank, `data/bank/` has one file per tournament and each is byte for byte what `tools/bank.js pack` writes, `index.json` maps every id (and every alias target) to its own tournament, every question's subject matches the subject its packet prints or is a listed override (see [Placement](#text-and-notation-conventions)), every image a record names exists under `data/img/` and every file there is used, the Visual filter splits the bank into visual and text-only questions with nothing left over, packet order matches a direct collator sort, and search returns the same matches as checking each word against each field separately. Run it after any data change.

`answer-check.js`, `data.js` and `mp-rules.js` attach to `globalThis` outside the browser, so tests `require` them directly.

**Browser tests** (`tests/e2e/`, about a minute: the files run four at a time, with Multiplayer split in two, and the CDN libraries are downloaded once per run; CI runs them in a second job): Play (buzzing, grading, the override, history cards and their shortcuts, Space with a focused card, Clear all filters, Clear stats, `n` and `s` each doing only its own job, the timer at 0 after a wrong interrupt, the alert when the last star has gone), the features from QB Reader in `qb-features.e2e.js` (the Database's Ignore word order, year slider and cards, the login page's eye button, Multiplayer subjects in Select by Set Name, question stats against a stand-in Supabase, self-graded Play, the year range in Play and Multiplayer, set and packet source links, frequency lists' downloads and pickers), Multiplayer with two simulated players (joining, settings kept in sync with and without network lag, overrides, the live chat line, the rounds message, room owners, mute and ban, controlled rooms, Allow skips, room links, join messages and the end of a set) and every page at desktop and phone widths. PeerJS is replaced by `tests/e2e/fake-peer.js`, which links the players' tabs through a BroadcastChannel (it can also add lag, or block direct links), and the service worker is off. Supabase is blocked except in one test, which blocks direct links and checks two players meet and stay in sync through the real project's Realtime relay (a broadcast channel; its database calls are blocked, so it leaves no rooms or players behind). Set `SB_E2E_OFFLINE=1` to skip it without a network. Further tests cover the update-after-redeploy check and the history card footer.
